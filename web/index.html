About

What this workbench does

This workbench inspects Cassini ISS Jupiter images for bright spots that might be lightning. The detector does not make a discovery claim. It creates a careful review queue so the science can be checked.

The goal is not to showcase AI. The goal is to develop a reliable method for finding, validating, and characterizing overlooked lightning candidates in Cassini data.

This is not confirmed lightning. These are candidates for review.

Cassini image brighten night side find bright spots remove obvious artifacts save candidates human review possible lightning
What goes in?

Calibrated Cassini ISS images, usually NAC/H-alpha frames from OPUS.

What comes out?

A candidate table with x/y position, brightness, blob size, local contrast, artifact flags, and a review score.

Who decides?

A human reviewer labels each candidate as known lightning, possible lightning, artifact, cosmic ray or hot pixel, or uncertain.

Scientific objective

Develop a validated pipeline that identifies candidate lightning events, minimizes false detections, and supports discovery and characterization of overlooked events.

Publishable path

Recover published flashes, keep false positives manageable, validate new candidates, then measure frequency, latitude, persistence, and color or spectral behavior.

Current science question

Can additional Cassini night-side images reveal overlooked storms, and can repeated detections help separate real lightning from cosmic rays and hot pixels?

    Plain-English detector diagram

    What the detector does

    Cassini image Brighten night side Find bright blobs Reject artifacts Save candidates Human review Possible lightning
    What image goes in?

    A calibrated Cassini ISS image of Jupiter's night side.

    What numbers are measured?

    x/y position, brightness, blob size, SNR/local contrast, artifact flags, and candidate score.

    What is a candidate?

    A bright blob worth reviewing. It is not confirmed lightning yet.

    What is a false positive?

    The detector flags something that is not lightning, like a cosmic ray or hot pixel.

    What is a false negative?

    The detector misses a real or published lightning spot.

    What is the score?

    A review priority ranking. It is not a probability unless it is calibrated later.

    Roadmap

    Build measurable output, then science

    Level 1 - engineering: run the current detector on Jan 1, Jan 10, Jan 11, and Jan 13, then save every candidate, including non-matches and likely artifacts. Level 2 - validation: recover published lightning, measure false positives and false negatives, and keep the review queue manageable. Level 3 - new candidates: label unmatched candidates as artifact, possible lightning, or uncertain. Do not call unmatched detections false automatically. Level 4 - science: use validated candidates to study frequency, latitude distribution, storm persistence, temporal evolution, and color or spectral behavior. Next scientific feature: map x/y pixels to Jupiter latitude/longitude. The same storm at the same planet location across frames or days is stronger evidence than one bright spot. Spectrum path: once candidates are stable, connect H-alpha detections to nearby broadband/filter images so lightning color and spectrum can be tested instead of guessed. Later tooling: compare classical CV, YOLO, or other models only after reviewed labels exist. The paper is about Jupiter, not the tool.
