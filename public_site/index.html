Cassini Jupiter Lightning Detection

Find candidates first. Confirm science second.

This project screens Cassini ISS Jupiter images for possible lightning candidates. The detector proposes bright regions; human review decides what is scientifically credible.

Safe claim

The pipeline generates reviewable candidate detections and validates against published lightning locations.

Unsafe claim

It has not yet confirmed newly discovered Jovian lightning without expert review and temporal/geometric validation.

Research Workflow

What happens to one Cassini image?

1 Cassini image

A calibrated image is selected from the OPUS/PDS dataset.

2 Enhance

The dark side is stretched so faint bright regions are easier to measure.

3 Detect blobs

The detector measures bright regions, not confirmed lightning.

4 Filter artifacts

Single-pixel events, streaks, and edge artifacts are flagged or rejected.

5 Score candidates

Each candidate receives measurements such as brightness, size, SNR, and shape.

6 Human review

A reviewer labels candidates as likely lightning, artifact, cosmic ray, or uncertain.

Current Scope

Processed review subset

The current local workflow has processed a focused 221-image Cassini ISS NAC/HAL subset across nine date windows. A broader OPUS query contains thousands of rows and still needs filtering before full detector processing.

Detector Type

Not YOLO yet

The current baseline is classical computer vision. YOLO or another model can be trained later after enough human-labeled candidate boxes exist.

Review Loop

How the model improves

Detector outputs become review packets. Human labels become training data. Future models are compared against the explainable baseline before scientific claims are made.

Reviewer-Friendly Plan

How collaborators can help without touching code

  1. The detector creates candidate crops and tables.
  2. A PDF review packet is generated for experts.
  3. Reviewers mark each candidate as positive, negative, or uncertain.
  4. The labels are entered into CSV/JSON files.
  5. Those labels become the training and validation set for future models.
